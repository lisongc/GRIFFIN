# User guide: finite SMEFT calculation

The public entry point is `griffin::prediction::SMEFTCalculator`. It combines
Lisong's coefficient inputs with Jackson's finite prescription, using:

> GRIFFIN's NNLO+ SM form factors and mixed electroweak–QCD-extended
> scattering amplitude, with dimension-six corrections incorporated through
> a finite, unlinearized prescription.

Here **NNLO+** means NNLO plus the selected higher-order SM terms implemented
in GRIFFIN. **Finite** means evaluating the prescription at the requested
Wilson coefficients, rather than extracting a derivative at zero. The
nonlinear terms generated by that prescription are retained; this does not
mean that every possible higher-power SMEFT contribution is included.

This is the current local integration. For code migration and dated test
results, see [MIGRATION.md](MIGRATION.md) and
[REGRESSION_TEST_REPORT.md](REGRESSION_TEST_REPORT.md).

**Scattering warning:** the unchanged `mat_SMaas` background has unresolved
behaviour in a neighbourhood of the real Z pole, not only at its exact
point. This is a collaborator-review calculation, not a validated precision
Z-lineshape implementation. Read the [warning and diagnostic](KNOWN_ISSUES.md#1-unresolved-near-pole-scattering-background)
before using scattering predictions.

## Preserved and changed

| Component | Merged choice |
|---|---|
| SM inputs | Existing `SMval` or `SMvalGmu`, with typed `InputPar` setters |
| Wilson coefficients | Lisong's separate `smeft::Input`, named setters, generation enums, zero defaults, explicit Lambda in GeV |
| Contact aliases | Cll current exchange and Cee current exchange/4D Fierz share canonical values; setters overwrite, never sum |
| Gmu mass | Jackson's finite fixed-point equation, including re-evaluation of SM Delta r at the shifted mass |
| FA and effective mixing angle | NNLO+ SM prediction at the resolved mass plus the explicit dimension-six shift, linear in coefficients at that fixed mass |
| FV | Constructed from those FA/SW values, retaining the existing SM imaginary-part term |
| Scattering amplitude | Existing `mat_SMaas` with shifted FA/SW, including its mixed electroweak–QCD residue/background extension, plus the matched contact amplitude exactly once |
| Cross section | Full angular square; no projection onto a linear coefficient |
| SMEFT calculation path | `SMEFTCalculator` only; no alternate linearized evaluator or SM-order selector |

The typed input structure and existing canonical contact strengths are
preserved. Equivalent Cll/Cee labels now address the same value; callers
that previously supplied independent raw entries must use the migration
rule below. There is no hardcoded SMEFT scale: replacing Lambda by
`k*Lambda` and every coefficient by `k*k*C` leaves the prediction unchanged.

## Exact prescription

Let $M$ denote the pole W mass and $M_Z$ the supplied pole Z mass. Define

$$
k=\frac{\pi\alpha}{\sqrt{2}G_\mu M_Z^2},\qquad
c_0^2=\frac12+\sqrt{\frac14-k},\qquad s_0^2=1-c_0^2,
$$

$$
\Delta_\mu=C_{\phi l,11}^{(3)}+C_{\phi l,22}^{(3)}
-\frac12C_{ll}^{1221},
$$

Here $C_{ll}^{1221}$ is the canonical total strength. The label $2112$
accesses that same value; it is not another term to sum in this equation.

$$
\delta x_6=-\frac{k}{2\sqrt{1/4-k}}
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}G_\mu\Lambda^2}
\left[2\frac{c_0}{s_0}C_{\phi WB}
+\frac{c_0^2}{2s_0^2}C_{\phi D}+\Delta_\mu\right].
$$

The Gmu mass is found by iterating Jackson's equation:

$$
M_{n+1}^2=M_Z^2\left[\frac12+
\sqrt{\frac14-k\bigl(1+\operatorname{Re}\Delta r_{\rm SM}(M_n)\bigr)}
+\delta x_6\right].
$$

The additive shift is **outside** the square root. This is not the old
linear mass displacement. The seed is 80 GeV and the default stopping
criterion is $|M_{n+1}^2-M_n^2|\leq10^{-4}\,{\rm GeV}^2$. As in Jackson's
code, the returned mass is the last evaluated iterate $M_n$, not the next
candidate. The iteration count and residual are available through
`massSolution()`. A configurable iteration limit and invalid-domain checks
replace an unbounded solve. The W width and running-mass/width values are
updated using Jackson's existing formula. In the alpha scheme the supplied
mass and widths are left unchanged.

At that resolved mass, the fixed-mass direct vertex shifts are denoted
$\delta a_f(C,M)$ and $\delta v_f(C,M)$. Then

$$
F_A^f=F_{A,\rm SM}^{f,\mathrm{NNLO+}}(M)+2a_{f,0}(M)\delta a_f(C,M),
$$

$$
s_{\rm eff}^{2,f}=s_{{\rm eff},\rm SM}^{2,f,\mathrm{NNLO+}}(M)
-\frac{\delta v_f/a_{f,0}-v_{f,0}\delta a_f/a_{f,0}^2}{4|Q_f|}.
$$

These explicit additions are linear in the coefficients **at fixed $M$**.
In particular, the FA input does not include an explicit $(\delta a_f)^2$,
and the mixing-angle input uses the displayed first-order ratio shift,
not the exact ratio of shifted vector and axial couplings. In Gmu, however,
$M=M(C)$ is itself solved at the requested coefficients, and all the SM
functions and direct shifts are evaluated there without expanding in
$M(C)-M(0)$.

No separate linear mass derivative is added. The functions
`smeft::directZAxialShift` and `smeft::directZVectorShift` compute only the
direct shift at the supplied mass. They have no input-scheme selector and do
not derive or shift MW. The calculator supplies the mass already resolved by
the mass resolver (`NativeInputPoint`), also in Gmu.

The requested FV construction is

$$
F_V^f=F_A^f\left[(1-4|Q_f|\operatorname{Re}s_{\rm eff}^{2,f})^2+I_f^2\right],
\qquad
I_f=\frac{a_{f,0}\operatorname{Im}v_{f,1}
-v_{f,0}\operatorname{Im}a_{f,1}}{a_{f,0}^2}.
$$

Here $I_f$ is GRIFFIN's existing SM imaginary-ratio term, evaluated at the
resolved mass. Keeping it preserves the `FV_SMNNLO` construction when all
Wilson coefficients vanish. This same FA/SW-based construction is used for
nonzero coefficients.

For each vector/axial channel,

$$
\mathcal A(C)=\mathcal A_{\tt mat\_SMaas}
\bigl(F_A(C),s_{\rm eff}^2(C);M(C)\bigr)+\mathcal A_{4f}(C).
$$

More explicitly, with $s_Z=M_Z^2-iM_Z\Gamma_Z$ and channel/angle labels
suppressed,

$$
\mathcal A(C;s)=
\frac{R_{\tt aas}\bigl(F_A(C),s_{\rm eff}^2(C);M(C)\bigr)}{s-s_Z}
+B_{\tt base}\bigl(s;M(C)\bigr)
+\delta B_{\tt aas}\bigl(s;M(C)\bigr)
+\mathcal A_{4f}(C).
$$

Here $R_{\tt aas}$ is the **complete** `mat_SMaas::coeffR()` residue,
not an extra correction to add to `mat_SMNNLO::coeffR()`.
$B_{\tt base}$ is `mat_SMNNLO::resoffZ()` and $\delta B_{\tt aas}$ is
`mat_SMaas::resoffZaas()`. The extension adds selected fermionic mixed
electroweak–QCD terms proportional to $\alpha\alpha_s$: additional imaginary
residue terms and energy-dependent photon/Z self-energy and photon–Z mixing
contributions. FA/SW already contain mixed electroweak–QCD corrections;
this is an additional amplitude-level contribution, not the first use of
$\alpha_s$ in the prediction.

The extension is evaluated at each calculation's resolved inputs for **both**
the requested-coefficient result and the zero-coefficient reference. The
reference repeats the Gmu mass solution with all coefficients set to zero;
it does not reuse the nonzero-coefficient W mass. In the alpha scheme both
evaluations use the same supplied W mass. At zero input $\alpha_s$, the
additional mixed electroweak–QCD scattering terms vanish.

The existing massless angular square is applied directly to these four
amplitudes. This includes SM–SMEFT interference, contact-squared terms and
vertex/contact products. Nonlinear dependence is also generated by the
finite Gmu solution and the pole/FV construction; it is not limited to a
single squared dimension-six amplitude. These generated terms do not supply
all higher-power terms of the SMEFT expansion.

### Reading the result

| Method | Meaning for an observable $O$ |
|---|---|
| `reference()` | $O(0)$: all Wilson coefficients zero, with the same prescription and a separate Gmu solve |
| `total()` | $O(C)$: the requested finite-coefficient prediction |
| `modelCorrection()` | $O(C)-O(0)$: the finite change, including the retained nonlinear dependence |

$O(C)$ denotes a function evaluated at $C$, not a claim that the answer is
first order in $C$. The finite change is neither a derivative at zero nor an
interference-only quantity. `FinitePrediction` has no inverse-scale order
labels.

## Contact inputs and importing Jackson benchmarks

For $e^+e^-\to\mu^+\mu^-$, the retained canonical-strength convention gives

$$
g_{LL}=C_{ll}^{1122}+C_{ll}^{1221},\quad
g_{RR}=C_{ee}^{1122},\quad
g_{LR}=C_{le}^{1122},\quad g_{RL}=C_{le}^{2211}.
$$

### Equivalent labels and setter behavior

For every generation tuple, Cll identifies the current-exchange pair
$prst\leftrightarrow stpr$. Thus $1122$ and $2211$ share one value, while
$1221$ and $2112$ share another; those two values remain independent.
Cee identifies the orbit $\{prst,stpr,ptsr,srpt\}$, using current exchange
and the four-dimensional same-chirality Fierz identity. In particular,
$1122,2211,1221,2112$ all name the same Cee value.

Each value is the total strength of one unique operator in this interface,
not a separately summed contribution for each spelling. Setters overwrite
that value: the latest assignment through any alias wins. Repeating an
assignment does not multiply the strength. No additional Hermiticity,
arbitrary index permutations or relations among other coefficients are
imposed. The pre-existing `Ceq`/reordered-`Cqe` alias is unchanged.

### Importing independent raw entries

To reproduce Jackson's four raw entries without changing physical strength,
first sum them outside the input object. The regression fixtures already
import them as

$$
C_{ll,L}^{1122}=C_{ll,J}^{1122}+C_{ll,J}^{2211},\qquad
C_{ll,L}^{1221}=C_{ll,J}^{1221}+C_{ll,J}^{2112},
$$

$$
C_{ee,L}^{1122}=C_{ee,J}^{1122}+C_{ee,J}^{1221}
+C_{ee,J}^{2211}+C_{ee,J}^{2112}.
$$

The Cle entries are unchanged and unrelated coefficients are zero. Each
canonical sum is assigned once; its alias getters then return the same sum.
Do not subsequently set an alias to zero or to one raw piece: that would
overwrite the imported total. Do not loop over all 81 raw tuples and set
unused aliases to zero. Start with a new zero-initialized `Input`, or call
`clearWilsonCoefficients()` to preserve Lambda while clearing coefficients;
then assign each unique strength once, after aggregating raw contributions.
The Gmu contribution is
$-\tfrac12 C_{ll,L}^{1221}
=-\tfrac12(C_{ll,J}^{1221}+C_{ll,J}^{2112})$, so the existing benchmark
normalization is preserved. No fixture values need to be rescaled.

This is an explicit conversion from Jackson's independently populated raw
entries, not summation performed by setters. No new public importer API is
provided. Existing already-canonical inputs need no change.

For example, migrate old independent values `Cll1221=a`, `Cll2112=b` to:

```cpp
const auto one = smeft::Generation::first;
const auto two = smeft::Generation::second;
coefficients.setCll(one, two, two, one, a + b); // assign the total once
```

Do not call `setCll(one,two,two,one,a)` followed by
`setCll(two,one,one,two,b)`: the second call replaces `a` with `b`; it does
not store `a+b`. Apply the same sum-once rule to the Cll current-exchange
pair $1122/2211$ and the four equivalent Cee labels when importing separate
raw contributions. See [SMEFT_IMPLEMENTATION.md](SMEFT_IMPLEMENTATION.md#contact-aliases-and-canonical-strengths)
for the precise supported identities.

## Using the merged API

The complete, compilable example is
[`examples/smeft_example.cc`](../examples/smeft_example.cc). Include the
public entry header, then fill the ordinary SM inputs as in that example:

```cpp
#include <griffin/SMEFT.h>

using namespace griffin;
// Construct and fill an SMvalGmu smGmu; see the complete example.
smeft::Input coefficients;
coefficients.setLambdaGeV(1000.0);
coefficients.setCphiWB(0.01); // dimensionless; C/Lambda^2 = .01 TeV^-2

prediction::ElectroweakSetup setup(EWInputScheme::GmuAlphaMZ, smGmu);
prediction::SMEFTCalculator calculation(setup, coefficients);
const Fermion initialFermion = Fermion::electron;
const Fermion finalFermion = Fermion::muon;
const double toPicobarns = 0.38937966e9;
prediction::ScatteringPoint point(initialFermion, finalFermion,
                                 90.0*90.0, 0.5, toPicobarns);
auto sigma = calculation.differentialCrossSection(point);
// sigma.reference(), sigma.total(), sigma.modelCorrection(), all in pb
auto fv = calculation.pseudoObservable(prediction::PseudoObservable::FV,
                                      Fermion::electron);
```

The point contains the initial/final fermions, $s$ in GeV$^2$ (not the energy
$\sqrt{s}$), $\cos\theta$, and an output-unit factor. The factor
`0.38937966e9` converts GeV$^{-2}$ to pb; the returned cross section is
$d\sigma/d\cos\theta$. To scan energy, call the same calculator with a new
`ScatteringPoint` at each energy. Changing only the kinematics does not
require a new coefficient or mass solution.

For alpha use an `SMval` input and `EWInputScheme::AlphaMWMZ`. The calculation
owns snapshots of both SM inputs and coefficients, so later changes to the
caller inputs cannot change an existing result. Construct a new calculation
for each fit point. No pointers into a caller's temporary inputs are retained.

The example's `ZMassExperiment` and `ZWidthExperiment` setters supply the
running-width input convention. `SMval`/`SMvalGmu` convert those inputs to
the `ZMassComplex` and `ZWidthComplex` pole parameters. The equations above
and the exact-$s=M_Z^2$ restriction use that pole mass, not the unconverted
91.1876 GeV input. The WMass observable likewise returns the resolved W
pole mass; the Gmu solver also supplies its running-width counterpart.

Build and run from the project root:

```bash
cmake -S . -B build/release -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release -DBUILD_TESTING=ON -DGRIFFIN_BUILD_EXAMPLES=ON
cmake --build build/release -j 4
ctest --test-dir build/release --output-on-failure
./build/release/examples/smeft_example
```

The numerical regression uses Python 3's standard library only. Its fixtures
are bundled in the test directory. Neither original source repository nor
an internet connection is needed to rerun the tests from this working copy.

## SM accuracy and public class names

The public classes are defined directly as `ElectroweakSetup` and
`SMEFTCalculator`, in `include/griffin/ElectroweakSetup.h` and
`include/griffin/SMEFTCalculator.h`. These are actual class names, not aliases.
See [MIGRATION.md](MIGRATION.md) for replacements of earlier class/header
names. Rebuild the library and its callers after a source-interface change.

`SMEFTCalculator` currently has **no selectable SM-order option**. It uses
the cumulative `FA_SMNNLO` and `SW_SMNNLO` form factors with `mat_SMaas`
for scattering; its Gmu solver uses `dr_SMNNLO`. The selected SM content
includes NNLO and selected three- and four-loop terms, not a uniform complete
order for every amplitude component. `mat_SMaas` includes the additional
mixed electroweak–QCD residue and off-resonance terms beyond the earlier
`mat_SMNNLO` comparison setup. The standalone FV construction is unchanged.

Consequently, current cross sections are not expected to reproduce the old
Jackson cross-section numbers unchanged. The archived fixtures remain
untouched: tests check the earlier assembly against that archive and the
current public result against a separately assembled `mat_SMaas` amplitude.
These checks share the underlying SM kernels and resolved mass inputs; they
are not an independent calculation of those kernels or a new Jackson
benchmark. See [the regression report](REGRESSION_TEST_REPORT.md).

There is no second calculation layer or hidden order selector. For SM-only
lower-level work, use the original SM classes directly; the retained SM
examples demonstrate that interface. SM accuracy and whether SMEFT products
are retained are separate decisions.

### Public headers and implementation

The active interface is now under `include/griffin/`:

- `SMEFT.h`: convenient entry header for ordinary users.
- `SMEFTCalculator.h`: finite SMEFT results, using the resolved mass and full square.
- `ElectroweakSetup.h` and `EWInputScheme.h`: the input scheme and supplied SM inputs.
- `Observables.h`: `PseudoObservable`, `ScatteringPoint` and `CurrentChannel`.

Implementation files are in `src/smeft/`, with the existing SM calculations
in `src/sm/`. The old top-level `prediction/`, `models/` and `smeft/` folders
are gone. The C++ namespace `griffin::prediction` remains unchanged; it is
not a directory or an additional engine. See the
[architecture guide](ARCHITECTURE.md) and [header migration table](MIGRATION.md#header-path-mapping).

When mixing the convenience header with advanced SM headers, include
`<griffin/SMEFT.h>` first. It loads the inline SM definitions before the
legacy input headers redefine mass macros. Use typed `InputPar` setters in
new code; they do not by themselves remove the legacy header-order issue
described in [known issues](KNOWN_ISSUES.md#5-ownership-and-legacy-error-handling).

## Scope and limitations

- The fixed SM choice is `FA_SMNNLO`, `SW_SMNNLO`, `mat_SMaas` and
  `dr_SMNNLO`. The amplitude extension is applied to both the full and SM
  reference predictions. It is not a claim of complete NNLO accuracy away
  from the resonance or a new SMEFT loop calculation.
- Real tree-level dimension-six shifts are inserted into the cumulative
  calculation. Its nonlinear products are retained, but this is not a
  complete higher-order SMEFT or dimension-eight calculation.
- Differential cross sections support massless electron annihilation to
  muons, taus, and the supported charged quarks. Bhabha and neutrino final
  states are rejected, as are scalar/pseudoscalar channels and collinear
  endpoint angles. No new scattering processes or real-radiation treatment
  were introduced.
- Exactly $s=M_Z^2$ (pole mass) is rejected, but this guard does not protect
  the nearby region: full-background checks find surviving single-pole and
  constant-residual double-pole terms in the unchanged `mat_SMaas` calculation,
  already at zero SMEFT coefficients. No universal safe exclusion window is
  established. The archived energies do not validate this region. Pole EWPOs
  remain available. See [the detailed warning](KNOWN_ISSUES.md#1-unresolved-near-pole-scattering-background).
- `DeltaR` is not exposed as a finite SMEFT observable: the additive
  mass-equation shift must not be confused with Delta r. WMass, FA, SW and FV are
  supported; SW for a neutral fermion is rejected.
- Nonphysical mass solutions, nonpositive FA, missing/nonfinite required
  inputs and nonfinite results raise exceptions. Physical-domain checks do
  not establish the validity of SM interpolation grids for arbitrary distant
  parameter choices or EFT validity for large coefficients.
- The historical linear SMEFT APIs are removed, not hidden behind an option.
  Migrate callers to `SMEFTCalculator`; there are no compatibility aliases
  for the removed prescription. The older SM classes remain available, with
  their separate ownership/error-handling limitations. The calculator does
  not use the legacy allocating FV/matrix constructors.
